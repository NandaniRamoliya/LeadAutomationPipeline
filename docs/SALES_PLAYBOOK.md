# Sales Playbook

The rules this pipeline follows, and what **you** should do on the parts a machine can't (calls, proposals,
pricing, closing). Distilled from *Sellbird Setup Kit*, *4 AI Employees*, *The Cold Email Hack Playbook*,
*The Client Onboarding System*, and standard freelance sales practice.

---

## 1. The three mistakes that kill freelance sales

1. **Waiting.** Portfolio up, hoping clients find you. They won't. You go to them. *(The engine finds leads every day.)*
2. **Copy-paste spam.** "Dear Sir/Madam, we provide website services…" to 500 people. *(Every email is built from that
   business's real problem; template phrases are blocked.)*
3. **No follow-up.** One message, no reply, give up. Most replies come from follow-ups. *(Five-step cold track, then
   separate tracks for every answer.)*

## 2. Find the trade before you write

Answer two questions for your niche:
- **What do I want?** A 15-minute call. Not a sale in the first email.
- **What do they get?** A specific problem fixed: more bookings, fewer missed enquiries, less wasted ad money.

Spend 90% of the effort on *who* and *why* (targets + signals) and 10% on the words. In this tool: choose targets and
signal weights carefully, and check the Leads page before approving anything.

**Signals = reasons to buy right now.** Good ones are visible, specific and costly:

| Signal | Why it sells |
|---|---|
| No online booking | Lost bookings every night and weekend |
| Not mobile-friendly / slow | Most local searches are on phones |
| Running ads + weak landing page | They're already spending; you stop the leak |
| Hiring | Growing pains = budget + urgency |
| No website / Instagram only | Invisible on Google |

## 3. The four principles of a winning cold email

1. **Credibility:** one relevant, provable result. *Show, don't claim.* ("Built booking sites for 12 clinics", not "expert web developer".)
2. **A specific ask:** one email, one ask. "Want the 2-minute audit?", not "explore synergies".
3. **Transparent intention:** say why you're writing. Ambition is fine; hidden intentions aren't.
4. **Extra effort:** offer something made for *them*: a mini audit video, a mock-up, three fixes, a teardown.

## 4. The formula (every first email)

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| Subject | 2–6 words, specific: "Quick question about {company}", "An idea for {company}" |
| Hook | One observation only *they* would recognise |
| Cost | What it costs them, in plain words, not your skills |
| Credibility | One line, relevant to their industry |
| Quick win | The free, small thing you'll make for them |
| Ask | One soft yes/no question |
| Length | Under ~80 words. No price, no links, no attachments |

## 5. Follow-ups that add something every time

Never "just checking in". Every follow-up brings one new thing:

| Day | What's new |
|---|---|
| 0 | The observation + offer |
| 3 | A second small observation |
| 7 | An insight: what you'd change first and why |
| 12 | The extra-effort offer made concrete ("reply yes and I'll make it") |
| 18 | A polite close; the door stays open |

Stop the moment someone declines or asks not to be contacted. *Persistence is not entitlement.*

## 6. The Closer: answering replies (every answer moves toward a 15-minute call)

| They say | You answer (the drafted reply does this) |
|---|---|
| **"How much?"** | Give a *range*, name the starter offer, ask for 15 minutes to give a fixed quote. Never refuse to answer. |
| **"Send your portfolio"** | 2–3 relevant links, then "happy to show how it would look for you on a quick call". |
| **"Not right now"** | "Totally understood. May I check back in a couple of months?" Then actually do (nurture track). |
| **"We already have someone"** | Respect it. Offer a free second opinion. No bashing the competition. |
| **"How long will it take?"** | Honest number + "I'll confirm a firm date after a quick call". |
| **"Interested, tell me more"** | Thank them, booking link, offer two time options if the link doesn't suit. |

**Speed matters:** answer hot replies within a few hours. The pipeline drafts the answer instantly and alerts you.

## 6b. WhatsApp, DMs and cold calls

- **DMs are shorter than emails:** 2–3 sentences, under 50 words, no links in the first one. Same formula: their
  problem, what it costs, the free quick win as a yes/no question.
- **WhatsApp:** business hours only, never voice notes or files first, never a second message the same day.
  If they don't answer, the follow-up comes days later with *something new*.
- **Cold call (2 minutes):** permission opener ("I'll be quick, do you have 30 seconds?") → the one problem you
  noticed → two questions → offer the free quick win → book 15 minutes. Talk less than they do.
- **Voicemail:** 20 seconds, name + one specific reason + "I'll send a short message". Then send it.
- **One "no" is enough** on any channel. The pipeline stops everywhere when someone says no or stop.

## 7. The 15-minute call

1. **Thank + agenda (1 min):** "I'll share what I found, ask a few questions, and if it makes sense, suggest next steps."
2. **Show the problem (3 min):** screen-share their site on a phone view. Let them see it.
3. **Ask (6 min):** How do bookings/enquiries come in today? How many per week? What happens to after-hours enquiries?
   Who decides? Budget range? When would you want this live?
4. **Suggest (3 min):** the starter offer as a first step. Outcome, timeline, price range.
5. **Next step (2 min):** "I'll send a one-page proposal today. Can we look at it together on Thursday?" Book it now.

## 8. Proposals and pricing

- One page: their problem (their words), the outcome, what's included, what's **not** included, timeline, price,
  payment terms, next step. Template: [templates/proposal.md](templates/proposal.md).
- Offer **3 options** (starter / recommended / complete). Most people choose the middle one.
- Anchor on value: "If this brings 3 extra bookings a week at ₹1,500 each, it pays for itself in two weeks."
- Discount scope, never price: remove items rather than lowering the number.
- Ask for 50% upfront (or 100% for small projects). **No payment, no onboarding.**

## 9. Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Too expensive" | "Understood. Which part matters most? We can start with just that." (smaller first step) |
| "Let me think about it" | "Of course. What would you need to see to feel sure?" |
| "Can you do it cheaper than X?" | "I'm probably not the cheapest. Here's what's included that makes the difference…" |
| "Send me more information" | Send one page, and book a 10-minute follow-up to go through it. |
| "My nephew does websites" | "Great! I'm happy to give him my audit notes." (goodwill, door stays open) |

## 10. After YES: onboarding (remove chaos before it starts)

Contract → Payment → Welcome document → Kickoff call → Access → Client portal. See [USER_GUIDE §12](USER_GUIDE.md#12-when-a-lead-says-yes-onboarding)
and [templates/](templates/).

- **If it's not written down, don't assume the client understands it.**
- Don't start delivery while commercial details are unclear.
- Collect access **once**, as user invites, never passwords on WhatsApp.
- One place for everything (the portal). If the client needs five places to track one project, the system is broken.

## 11. Keep clients, get referrals

The client-care track: check-in at 2 weeks, testimonial at 30 days, referral ask at 60 days, next project at 120 days.
Referrals close faster and pay better than any cold lead.

## 12. Rules never to break

1. Never email someone without understanding why you're contacting them.
2. Never make the email about yourself.
3. Never ask without considering what they get.
4. Never send generic AI-generated personalisation.
5. Never confuse persistence with entitlement.
6. Never put a price in the first message, never promise results you can't deliver, never message 100 people a day by hand.
7. Always check a lead before you message it.

## 13. Numbers to track (Analytics page)

| Metric | Healthy | If not |
|---|---|---|
| Bounce rate | < 3% | Stop. Fix verification; stop role/guessed emails |
| Reply rate | 5–15% | Targeting or first email is off |
| Positive reply rate | 2–5% | Offer or proof is weak |
| Calls booked / positive replies | > 40% | Reply faster, make booking easier |
| Won / calls | 20–40% | Work on the call and the proposal |

**The 30-day challenge:** ~100 well-chosen people, ~20 personal emails a week per mailbox (more as you warm up),
5 custom quick-wins a week, follow up with everyone, review the numbers weekly. Measure what comes out, not what goes in.
